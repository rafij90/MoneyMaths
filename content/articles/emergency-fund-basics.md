An emergency fund is cash set aside for necessary costs that are hard to predict. It is not meant to maximize returns; its first job is to be available when life becomes expensive.

## Start with essential expenses

List the monthly costs you would keep paying during an income gap: housing, food, utilities, insurance, transport, and minimum debt payments. Leave optional spending out of this first estimate.

## Choose a runway

Three to six months of essential expenses is a common starting range. A variable income, a single household earner, or a higher risk of large repairs may justify a larger buffer.

## Keep it accessible

Use an account that is safe and easy to access, separate from your everyday spending account. Once the fund is complete, redirect the same monthly contribution toward longer-term goals. The [emergency fund calculator](../calculators/emergency-fund.html) can turn your expense list into a target.
