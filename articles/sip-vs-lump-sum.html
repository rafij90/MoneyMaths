SIP vs Lump Sum: Which Wins?

Compare regular investing with a lump-sum investment and learn when each approach may fit your goals.

SIP and lump-sum investing are two ways to put money into the market. The better choice depends on your cash flow, time horizon, and comfort with market timing.

How a SIP works

A systematic investment plan invests a fixed amount at regular intervals. When prices fall, the same contribution buys more units; when prices rise, it buys fewer. This spreads your entry points across time.

How a lump sum works

A lump sum invests available cash immediately. It gets more time in the market, which can help when markets rise, but the full investment is exposed to the market level on the day you invest.

A practical decision

Use a SIP when your income arrives regularly or you want a repeatable habit. Consider a lump sum when you already have the money, have a long time horizon, and can tolerate short-term changes in value. Compare both approaches with the lump-sum calculator and SIP calculator.
